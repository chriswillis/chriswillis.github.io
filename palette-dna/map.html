Palette DNA Map

See a palette's shape: where it has published colors, where the engine fills in between them, and how well each hue can be rebuilt from its neighbors. For people who want to study a palette before borrowing it. To make a ramp, use Palette DNA Ramp.

Why borrow a palette's shape

Formula generators apply the same rule at every hue. That keeps ramps consistent, but it loses the hue-by-hue decisions in palettes their designers authored. In our tests, Material 3, which is generated from a formula, was far easier to rebuild than designer-authored palettes such as Flexoki: a median error of 0.34 against 2.27 ΔE OK ×100, where about 2 is one just-noticeable difference. Not every authored palette is hard to predict: Radix scored 0.82.

This engine reads a palette's shape and fills in the hue you need. We hid each of 149 published ramps and rebuilt it from one of its colors and the palette's other ramps. 65% of the rebuilt steps were within one just-noticeable difference of what the designers shipped, against 52% for copying the nearest ramp. How to read the map

Hue position runs across; lightness runs up. The picker uses PCHIP independently of the 3D interpolation controls. Solid lines show lofted ramps; dashed lines show interior ramps. Stippling is a reference from full-saturation PCHIP hold-outs, plus areas beyond neighbouring ramps' lightness ranges. It does not validate a seed-shaped ramp or predict an individual color's error. Error-based stippling is hidden when palette saturation changes; extrapolation stippling remains. Hatching marks gamut clipping.

Import a palette

  
Paste CSS custom properties (--red-500: #e11d48;), SCSS or LESS variables, JSON (Tailwind-style objects, arrays or design tokens), or one ramp per line (red: #fee #fcc #f88 #e44). Colors can be hex, rgb(), hsl(), oklch(), oklab() or color(display-p3 …). Ramps need at least 3 steps; you need at least 4 hued ramps. Low-chroma ramps become neutral axes.
