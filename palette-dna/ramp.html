Why borrow a palette's shape

    
Formula generators apply the same rule at every hue. That keeps ramps consistent, but it loses the hue-by-hue decisions in palettes their designers authored. In our tests, Material 3, which is generated from a formula, was far easier to rebuild than designer-authored palettes such as Flexoki: a median error of 0.34 against 2.27 ΔE OK ×100, where about 2 is one just-noticeable difference. Not every authored palette is hard to predict: Radix scored 0.82.

    
This tool reads a palette's shape and fills in the hue you need. We hid each of 149 published ramps and rebuilt it from one of its colors and the palette's other ramps. 65% of the rebuilt steps were within one just-noticeable difference of what the designers shipped, against 52% for copying the nearest ramp. When not to use it

    
    
      
  • You need every step to meet a contrast target: use Harmony or Leonardo.

  • You need dark-mode tokens: not supported yet.
    • 
      
  • Your palette has fewer than four colored ramps: the tool needs neighboring hues to interpolate between.
    • 
      
  • You need a guaranteed categorical chart scheme: companion suggestions are a starting point. Inspect the actual chart colors and use labels or shapes alongside color.

How it works

    
Each reference palette is treated as a shape. Its ramps are wrapped around a surface in Oklab, a color space where equal distances look about equally different. Your color is placed on that surface by its hue, lightness and colorfulness. The new ramp follows the surface: the same lightness steps, and the same rise and fall in colorfulness, as the palette's own ramps at that hue.

    
Through color shapes the ramp around your color. Gamut limits, neutral substitution and shared anchor steps can change or omit an input; the result reports those changes. Fits the palette tones a color down if it is more vivid than the palette ever goes at that hue, so the ramp matches its siblings.

    
Ramp shape. Custom resamples every ramp to 3–32 stops. Each ramp keeps its own lightest and darkest values unless you set a range, and colorfulness is carried as a share of what the gamut allows at each new lightness. A custom shape left at its starting values returns the reference steps unchanged. Your color stays an exact stop unless you turn that off; a range that excludes it is widened and the result says so.

    
Color sets. Chart color sets such as Tableau 20 have too few steps per hue to act as a reference, so they work as starting colors instead. Add a color set builds one ramp per color, shaped like your reference palette and kept through that color and, where it fits, its lighter pair. The chart preview starts on the set’s own colors. Tableau 20 values are from Tableau’s 2016 redesign and belong to Tableau.

    
Your own palette. Choose Import your palette in the Shape it like menu, then paste or upload your color tokens. It needs at least four color ramps that share the same step names. The page validates the colors and keeps the palette in this browser only.

    
The numbers above come from hiding each real ramp, rebuilding it from its most colorful step, and comparing the result with the shipped ramp. Reported input differences use ΔE OK ×100; zero means the same internal color. Hex exports round to 8-bit sRGB; use CSS oklch for higher precision and wide-gamut colors.
