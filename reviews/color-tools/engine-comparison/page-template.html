Hide a ramp, seed with its own color, regenerate

A comparison of the topology engine in the two color pages with the unshipped palette-dna library. For every lofted ramp in eleven palettes, the ramp is removed, its most colorful step is used as the seed, and each engine rebuilds the ramp from the rest. Every other step is then compared with what the palette's designers shipped.

Error is ΔE OK ×100. About 2 is one just-noticeable difference. Both engines receive exactly the same palette data (the copy embedded in color-ramp.html). Kendo Meridian is shown but left out of the pooled numbers: it has five chart steps per hue, not a ramp. {{N}} ramps in the pooled set.

What this shows

Small leadThe topology engine is closer to what designers shipped, but by about 0.2 ΔE at the median. Neither engine's way of reading the palette is clearly wrong.
Copying a neighbor is closeRotating the nearest remaining ramp to the seed's hue gets within 0.5 of both engines at the median. Topology's gain shows up mostly in the worst steps (90th percentile 4.05 against 4.76). palette-dna's worst steps are worse than the baseline's (5.00).
Even spacing costs fidelitypalette-dna's default respacing moves steps away from where designers put them. The median rises from 1.45 to 2.40, and Radix goes from 1.05 to 13.2. This measures fidelity to the source, not quality: even steps may be a better ramp.
The step is the big errorBoth engines keep the seed color exactly. But when an engine puts it on a different step from the designer's, the rest of the ramp shifts, and that ramp's error more than doubles.

Material 3 is generated from a formula rather than hand-tuned, and it is by far the easiest to predict. Flexoki and Atlassian are hardest for both engines, and Open Color is hard for palette-dna. That fits the idea that hand-tuning adds character a model cannot fully predict, but it is not proof: Radix is hand-tuned and still easy to predict.
